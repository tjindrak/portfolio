vyzva:
  nadpis: The Cold War on a single table.
  odstavce:
    - "The game was my very first end-of-term project at VŠKK, supervised by Martin Strnad of HMS Design studio. The brief was simple: build a board game from scratch that actually works. We borrowed the atmosphere from indie games like Papers, Please! and moved the story to the border between East and West Berlin."
    - Players act as border guards under time pressure, checking passports, spotting mistakes, answering history questions and completing tasks. By the end we had a finished game with its own cards, currency, game board and rules.

proces:
  nadpis: Berlin, based on real plans.
  odstavce:
    - My main job in the team was the game board, which was mine from start to finish. I put it together from real plans of Berlin, satellite images and every map I could find. I vectorised the map and adjusted it by hand to fit our needs. I drew every location to scale so it matched reality while still making sense for the flow of the game. I placed the checkpoints so they followed on from each other logically, and the mechanics grew along with them.

reseni:
  nadpis: From a school project to the publishers.
  slovo: "Solution and impact"
  odstavce:
    - "It was meant to be “just” a school assignment, but the project grew into something bigger. At VŠKK it earned top marks for originality and complexity. Over the next 14 days we redrew the artwork, had a professional prototype made and got a unique opportunity:"
    - "> “We presented the whole concept to the publishers Albi and Dino.”"
    - In the end, the game didn’t go into production. The experienced publishers found the core mechanics a little dated, and today’s market calls for a different kind of play. For me and the whole team, though, it was incredibly valuable real-world feedback.
    - It was my first big test of designing a complex product from scratch under time pressure. I learned to think not just about the graphics, but above all about how players feel at the table and how the rules shape their behaviour. For the first time, I pitched my own idea to major players in the market and understood how big the gap is between a “nice idea” and a finished product for the mass market.
